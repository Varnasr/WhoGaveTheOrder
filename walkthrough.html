Steps 1–4

The two-minute version

01

This archive asks one question

When state power is used against citizens, who authorised it?

Not whether it happened, not whether it was justified. Who signed the order, or gave it aloud, and what record exists of that. Everything on this site is organised around getting to a document that answers it.

02

Most of it says UNKNOWN, and that is the finding

If you open an investigation you will see black Unknown blocks where a date, an injury figure or a named official would normally sit. That is not a page that failed to load.

A claim without a source does not render as a fact here, including in our own starting data. Where we have not established something, the site says so rather than filling the space with a plausible reconstruction.

Unknown

No order has been located.

This is what an honest empty record looks like. It is a statement about the archive, not a claim about anyone.

03

Every statement carries one of six labels

These six words carry the site's credibility. A claim cannot appear without one, and moving a claim between them requires a document.

Hover or tap any label anywhere on the site to see its definition. They are defined once, in one file, so a label on one page cannot mean something different on another.

04

Holding an office is not evidence of giving an order

The chain of command maps who is empowered to decide what. That is a statement about law and administrative structure. Whether a particular office actually authorised a particular action is a different claim, and it needs a document.

The interface keeps them in two separate boxes and never merges them:

Has authority

Empowered on the face of the law to take decisions of this kind. Says nothing about what it did.

Evidenced to have authorised this action

Not established. No document in the archive places this office behind this specific action.

This is why no individual is named anywhere in the chain. A person is added only when a sourced document places them in an office at the relevant time.

Steps 5–9

The full walkthrough

05

Where every claim comes from

Nothing on this site is allowed to skip a link in this chain:

Incident → Claim → Evidence → Source

A source record is created first, because nothing can cite what does not exist. An evidence record points at that source and carries two written lines: what it establishes, and what it does not. Only then can a claim move off UNKNOWN, listing the evidence IDs that carry it.

Every evidence item keeps a permanent ID like WGO-0001. Claims cite the ID rather than restating the document, so you are always one click from the thing itself.

06

What it does not establish matters as much

Every evidence record answers two questions, and the second is the harder one.

What this establishes

A reply confirming a record exists or does not exist is primary evidence, and is filed as such.

What this does not establish

A department stating no record exists does not establish that no order was given.

A document that proves less than it appears to is the most common way an archive like this goes wrong. Writing the limit down is how it is avoided.

07

Open questions, and the clock on each one

Every gap is written as a specific question with a status, and each names the document that would answer it. The gap list is the whole backlog in one table.

When we request a document, the RTI register starts a clock. Filing a statutory information request puts a public office on a deadline, and the register shows that deadline running:

RTI-0001Overdue by 17 days

Filed 29 June, reply was due 29 July.

An office that has not answered in 40 days is a recorded fact with a date on it. Silence past the statutory period counts as a refusal, and it opens an appeal.

08

How to check our work

You do not have to take any of this on trust.

  • The editorial console is public. It runs integrity checks on every page load and shows what the archive currently fails.
  • The archive is kept in a public version-controlled repository, so who changed what, and when, is checkable without asking us.
  • The government response page puts our evidence, the official account and the unanswered questions in three columns, and never collapses them into a verdict.
  • Every record carries a report an error route. Corrections are logged with the date and the reason.

09

What you can do

The archive fills up one document at a time, and most of them are held by ordinary people rather than by anyone in particular.

  • If you hold something — a photograph off the device that took it, a notice, a reply from an office — send it. Nothing you send goes live automatically.
  • If you can file a request — pick a line off the gap list and file it yourself. Send us the reference number and we will track the clock publicly, under your name or anonymously.
  • If you have published on this already — send the link and it goes in the index of other people's work, credited to you.

Worth knowing before you cite us

What this site will not do

  • It will not tell you what happened. There is no narrative account anywhere on this site. It holds documents and the state of each claim. If you want a story, this is the wrong format, and deliberately so.
  • It will not name a person because they held a post. That is how an investigation turns into an allegation, and how it gets dismissed.
  • It will not hide an inconvenient official response. Statements are reproduced in full, including the ones that cut against us.
  • It will not treat UNKNOWN as a problem to be cleared. Reducing that count by inference would defeat the point of keeping it.
