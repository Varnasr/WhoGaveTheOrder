What we have asked, and who has not answered

The RTI register

Every application this archive has filed, the office it went to, and the date a reply was due. Applications appear here on the day they are filed, not the day an answer arrives.

Why an unanswered application is published

Filing an RTI puts a statutory clock on a public office. When that clock runs out without a reply, the Act treats the silence as a refusal, and the applicant gains a right of appeal.

So an application sitting 40 days overdue is not a gap in this archive. It is a recorded fact about a public office, with a date on it, and it belongs in the register exactly as much as a reply does.

What each outcome means here

  • Reply received — answered on the merits. The reply is filed as evidence.
  • No record held — the office says it holds nothing. Filed as evidence of what the office has said about its own files. It does not establish that no order was given.
  • Refused — an exemption was claimed. The exemption cited is recorded.
  • Transferred — sent on to another authority, which narrows where the record sits.
  • No reply — the statutory period passed in silence.

Filing one yourself. Anyone can file an RTI application, and you do not need our permission or involvement to do it.
