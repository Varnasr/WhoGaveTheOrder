Why that matters.

Police can use force. There are rules about who may authorise it, and those rules require somebody to decide, and often to write it down.

The number of offices empowered to make that decision is not zero. What is zero is the number of documents showing that any of them did. An office with the power to order something is not an office shown to have ordered it, and this site does not treat the first as evidence of the second anywhere.

The records that would settle it are held by district police offices and kept on a retention schedule, so they are erased on a timetable whether or not anyone asks.

How it works. Three steps.

01

Someone asks

A document is requested from the office that holds it, or sent in by someone who has it. The request goes on the public list the day it is filed.

02

It gets checked

Against its original source. Then it is filed with two lines: what it proves, and what it does not. Nothing is published on trust.

03

The record moves

A question changes state only when a document changes it. Silence from an office is recorded too, with the date it fell due.

What we are looking for.

Somebody filed it, carried it, was briefed from it, or received a copy.
