Transparency

How this site decides what is credible

A project that asks public offices to explain themselves should be able to explain how it works. Everything below is written for a reader who thinks we are wrong.

Who runs this project

An unfunded research project, founded and run by three people on their own time.

  • Dr Varna Sri Raman — independent development economist, All India Professionals' Congress
  • Syed Khalid Saifullah — president, Hyderabad District Congress Committee
  • Pallavi Nayak — All India Professionals' Congress

Those descriptions are the disclosure. Nothing about who runs this changes any rule the site runs on: nothing appears here as a fact without a source attached, every claim carries a state — verified, corroborated, reported, disputed, unverified, unknown — with the definitions published, an automated check refuses a claim with no source, another warns when everything marked corroborated traces back to a single origin, and every change is retained with the commit it landed in.

A record that is inconvenient to us is filed to the same standard as one that is not. The ten FIRs and the injured police personnel of 22 July are in this archive at INC-DEL-003 for exactly that reason.

The test is not who we are, it is whether the records hold. Every one of them names its source and links to it. Read those rather than this paragraph, and if a record overstates what its source says, write to corrections@whogavetheorder.in and it will be corrected in public with the date.

People who send documents to this archive are never named unless they ask to be. Nothing sent in is published automatically, and that rule is not affected by anything on this page.

Journalists and lawyers can write to evidence@whogavetheorder.in.

Funding

None. There is no funding, no grant and no donor. The archive is built and maintained on unpaid time.

That bears on capacity as much as on independence. This archive moves at the pace three people with other jobs can file information requests and read the replies, which is why the gap list is long and why someone else filing a request is the single most useful contribution available.

Editorial independence

Four commitments, chosen because each can be tested against the archive itself:

  • No funder, party or organisation sees a record before it is published, or is told what is in one, whoever they are to us.
  • No party's conduct is exempt from the record, and no party's conduct is the record's purpose. This archive documents police forces under governments of whatever complexion; where a state government is a respondent, its account is published in full at the same prominence as anyone else's.
  • Evidence that cuts against the project's own framing is published at the same prominence as evidence that supports it. The government response page exists for that reason.
  • No claim is upgraded to VERIFIED because it is politically useful, or left at UNVERIFIED because it is politically awkward. A claim's state follows from the documents attached to it.

Methodology

The archive is built on a fixed chain, and no page is allowed to skip a link in it:

Incident → Claim → Evidence → Source

and, in parallel:

Incident → Question → Authority → Evidence → Answer state

What that means in practice

  • A source record is created first. Nothing can cite what does not exist.
  • An evidence record points at that source and carries two written statements: what it establishes, and what it does not. Both are required.
  • A claim may only be set to VERIFIED or CORROBORATED once evidence IDs are attached to it. The site's own integrity check flags any claim that breaks this, and that check is visible on the editorial console.
  • A question moves out of UNANSWERED only when a document moves it, not when an inference becomes obvious.
  • An office is never linked to an order automatically. Has authority and is evidenced to have authorised this action are separate fields, displayed separately.

Where this archive is weak

  • It depends on documents that public offices are not obliged to release quickly, and on RTI timelines that outlast news cycles.
  • Oral orders leave no paper. Where an order was given orally, the most this archive can establish may be who was present and what was logged.
  • Submitted footage is often a copy of a copy, stripped of the metadata that would fix its time and place. Such material stays UNVERIFIED, sometimes permanently.

Verification standards

These six words, and the four question states, are used strictly. They are defined once in the site's taxonomy file, and this page reads those definitions from the same file the rest of the site does, so the two cannot drift apart.

Source policy

  • A primary document beats a report of one. Where we hold only the report, the state is REPORTED and stays there.
  • A document is checked against its issuing office or registry where that is possible. Where it is not, the record says so.
  • Anonymous sources are used and recorded as anonymous. An anonymous account alone does not lift a claim past UNVERIFIED.
  • We do not pay for evidence.
  • We do not publish a contributor's identity unless they have ticked the box asking us to.

Corrections

Every evidence record carries a Report an error link. When a record changes materially we log the change, its date and its reason, and the record shows that it was corrected.

The archive is kept in a version-controlled repository, so every change to every record is retained with its date and its reason. That repository is currently private, because it also holds the queue of documents this project is about to request and from which offices. The evidence data itself is available on request to anyone who wants to check the working: journalists, lawyers, researchers, or an office named in the archive. Write to evidence@whogavetheorder.in.

If we have something wrong, write to corrections@whogavetheorder.in with the record ID.

Privacy, takedown and risk

What this site does to you

Nothing runs in your browser. There are no cookies, no tracking scripts, no pixels, no fingerprinting and no advertising. No account is needed and none is offered. Nothing is written to your device.

There is no cookie banner because there are no cookies to consent to. A banner here would imply a kind of tracking that does not happen.

How visits are counted. Like every web server, the one serving this site records the requests it receives. Aggregate counts are read from those logs — how many people opened a page, which pages, what linked to them. That reading happens on the server after the fact. No script reports on you, nothing identifies you between visits, and no analytics company is given your data. This distinction is the whole point: counting how many people read a page is not the same as following a person around.

Two exceptions, both on the map page only. The incident map on the investigations page loads its mapping library from unpkg.com and its map tiles from CARTO. Those two companies therefore see your IP address and the fact that you loaded a map, in the same way any website that embeds a map does. No other page on this site makes a third-party request. If that matters to you, every other page works without it, and the map degrades to a plain list of locations.

Contributors

  • Contact details are stored privately, are never published, and are never shared.
  • A submission is not public. It becomes public only if it is verified, and only if the contributor gave permission to publish.
  • Contributors may withdraw a submission at any point before publication, and may ask for it to be removed after publication.

People in the material

  • Faces of protesters, detainees and bystanders are blurred on request, and by default where a person appears to be a minor or is visibly injured.
  • Medical detail is not published without the consent of the person it concerns.
  • Police personnel acting in a public capacity are treated as public actors. Their families are not.

Takedown

A takedown request may be sent to legal@whogavetheorder.in by anyone, including a public office. Every request gets a written reply. Where a request is well-founded, whether a privacy risk, a copyright claim from the creator, or a person at risk of harm, the material comes down and the record notes what was removed and why.

Where a request is not well-founded we refuse it in writing and log the request. An attempt to remove sourced material from a public archive is itself part of the record.

Reuse and licence

The point of an archive is that other people use it. Journalists, lawyers, researchers and other archives are welcome to take what is here.

  • Records written by this project — the claims, the questions, the chain-of-command mapping, the two lines on each document saying what it establishes and what it does not — may be reused with attribution to this archive and a link back.
  • Documents obtained from public offices, such as replies to information requests, court records and orders, are public records. This project claims no rights over them.
  • Other people's work indexed under others are investigating belongs to whoever made it. Their terms govern, not ours. We link; we do not relicense.
  • Material sent in by contributors is published only at the permission level the contributor gave, and is not available for onward relicensing by us.

If you want to reuse something and are unsure which of those it falls under, ask.

What this site is not

It is not legal advice, and it is not a substitute for a court record. Nothing here establishes liability. A claim marked VERIFIED means a primary document supports it, which is a statement about the document and not a finding against any person.

The state of this archive

The archive holds sourced records and is being added to. Dates and locations are established for the Delhi and Bihar incidents; Maharashtra is held open with no incident located. Beyond those, most factual fields still read UNKNOWN, and the chain of authority is mapped by statutory role rather than by evidence of who acted.

No injury figure and no named official has been entered, because neither has been verified. No order authorising the use of force has been found. There are no layout samples on the site any more: everything shown is a real record.

The machinery is what makes the rest possible: the content model, the evidence grammar, the integrity checks, and the rule that a claim without a source does not display as a fact. The gaps are the work, not a defect in it.
