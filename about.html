Transparency

How this site decides what is credible

A project that asks public offices to explain themselves should be able to explain itself. Everything below is written for a reader who thinks we are wrong.

Every source of money behind the project, and the amount, disclosed by name. Any donor with a political affiliation described as such. If the project is unfunded and run on volunteer time, state that too: it bears on the archive's capacity as well as its independence.

Editorial independence

Three commitments, chosen because each can be tested against the archive itself:

  • No funder, party or organisation sees a record before it is published, or is told what is in one.
  • Evidence that cuts against the project's own framing is published at the same prominence as evidence that supports it. The government response page exists for that reason.
  • No claim is upgraded to VERIFIED because it is politically useful, or left at UNVERIFIED because it is politically awkward. A claim's state follows from the documents attached to it.

Methodology

The archive is built on a fixed chain, and no page is allowed to skip a link in it:

Incident → Claim → Evidence → Source

and, in parallel:

Incident → Question → Authority → Evidence → Answer state

What that means in practice

  • A source record is created first. Nothing can cite what does not exist.
  • An evidence record points at that source and carries two written statements: what it establishes, and what it does not. Both are required.
  • A claim may only be set to VERIFIED or CORROBORATED once evidence IDs are attached to it. The site's own integrity check flags any claim that breaks this, and that check is visible on the editorial console.
  • A question moves out of UNANSWERED only when a document moves it, not when an inference becomes obvious.
  • An office is never linked to an order automatically. Has authority and is evidenced to have authorised this action are separate fields, displayed separately.

Where this archive is weak

  • It depends on documents that public offices are not obliged to release quickly, and on RTI timelines that outlast news cycles.
  • Oral orders leave no paper. Where an order was given orally, the most this archive can establish may be who was present and what was logged.
  • Submitted footage is often a copy of a copy, stripped of the metadata that would fix its time and place. Such material stays UNVERIFIED, sometimes permanently.

Verification standards

These six words, and the four question states, are used strictly. They are defined once in the site's taxonomy file, and this page reads those definitions from the same file the rest of the site does, so the two cannot drift apart.

Source policy

  • A primary document beats a report of one. Where we hold only the report, the state is REPORTED and stays there.
  • A document is checked against its issuing office or registry where that is possible. Where it is not, the record says so.
  • Anonymous sources are used and recorded as anonymous. An anonymous account alone does not lift a claim past UNVERIFIED.
  • We do not pay for evidence.
  • We do not publish a contributor's identity unless they have ticked the box asking us to.

Corrections

Every evidence record carries a Report an error link. When a record changes materially we log the change, its date and its reason, and the record shows that it was corrected.

The archive is kept in a public version-controlled repository, so the full history of every record is public. Who changed what, and when, is checkable without taking our word for it.

If we have something wrong, write to corrections@whogavetheorder.in with the record ID.

Privacy, takedown and risk

Contributors

  • Contact details are stored privately, are never published, and are never shared.
  • A submission is not public. It becomes public only if it is verified, and only if the contributor gave permission to publish.
  • Contributors may withdraw a submission at any point before publication, and may ask for it to be removed after publication.

People in the material

  • Faces of protesters, detainees and bystanders are blurred on request, and by default where a person appears to be a minor or is visibly injured.
  • Medical detail is not published without the consent of the person it concerns.
  • Police personnel acting in a public capacity are treated as public actors. Their families are not.

Takedown

A takedown request may be sent to legal@whogavetheorder.in by anyone, including a public office. Every request gets a written reply. Where a request is well-founded, whether a privacy risk, a copyright claim from the creator, or a person at risk of harm, the material comes down and the record notes what was removed and why.

Where a request is not well-founded we refuse it in writing and log the request. An attempt to remove sourced material from a public archive is itself part of the record.

The status of this build

A working prototype of the platform, not a launched archive. It ships empty of factual assertions: the three investigations exist as shells, the questions are populated, the chain of authority is mapped by statutory role, and every factual field reads UNKNOWN.

No date, no injury figure, no named official and no evidence item has been entered, because none has been verified. Records marked sample show the layout only, and disappear when the archive's demonstration flag is switched off.

What works today is the machinery: the content model, the evidence grammar, the integrity checks, and the rule that a claim without a source does not display as a fact.
