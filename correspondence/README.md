# Correspondence

Drafts of letters this project sends to public authorities, and a dated record of
what was sent and when.

**Nothing in this folder has been sent.** A letter moves from draft to sent only
when a person sends it, and the despatch date is then recorded here. An
accountability archive that cannot say what it asked for, and when, is asking
other people for a standard it does not keep.

## What these are, and what they are not

A preservation representation is **not a statutory instrument**. It compels
nothing. Its value is narrower and worth stating plainly: once an authority has
been told in writing that identified records are the subject of an inquiry and a
pending information request, the destruction of those records after that date is
itself a documentable fact, with a date on it. That is the whole mechanism.

They are also not RTI applications, so they do not enter the RTI register on
`rti.html`, which tracks applications with a statutory clock running. These have
no clock. `FQ-002` is the RTI that follows.

## Before sending

1. **Fill every `[ ]` placeholder.** Sender name, address and contact. The
   drafts leave these blank rather than invent them.
2. **Check the office address and the name of the current officeholder.** This
   archive holds neither, and neither is guessed here. A letter to a
   misidentified officer is a letter that can be ignored for a good reason.
3. **Read the incident paragraph against the records.** Each letter states only
   what the archive can source, and says so where it cannot. The Munger letter
   in particular does **not** assert that force was used, because the archive
   does not establish that (`INC-BIH-003`, `UNVERIFIED`). Do not "tidy" that
   into a firmer claim.
4. **Keep proof of despatch.** Speed post receipt, or the sent record and any
   delivery receipt if sent by email. The proof is the point.
5. **Record the despatch date here** afterwards, in `sent.md`, one line per
   letter.

## Email is now a usable route

The domain's SPF record was corrected and, checked on 18 September 2026, reads
`v=spf1 include:secureserver.net include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all`
against MX on `whogavetheorder-in.mail.protection.outlook.com` and DMARC at
`p=quarantine`. Mail from the project's own addresses will now pass SPF at a
receiving government mail system, which it would not have done before. Post
remains better where an address is known, because a speed post receipt is
stronger evidence of despatch than a sent-items entry.
