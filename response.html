How we treat an official denial

A denial is evidence. It is on the record, it is attributable, and it commits an office to a position that documents can later confirm or contradict. We publish it as carefully as anything else.

What a denial is not is an answer. "No excessive force was used" does not tell you who authorised the deployment. This page keeps that distinction visible in the third column.

When an official reply says no record exists

A reply stating that no record exists is filed as primary evidence, because it is one. It establishes what a public office has said about its own files.

It does not establish that no order was given. Orders are given orally. Records are also mislaid, misfiled and withheld. The archive records the reply, states what it establishes, and leaves the underlying question open.
